What actually changes how people understand AI.

We run values‑segmented research that tests which messages move which audiences, and maps who they already trust to deliver them.

How we research

  • 01A values‑segmented survey: grouping people by underlying values and worldview, not demographics alone
  • 02A message test: putting real claims about AI in front of each segment to see what actually shifts understanding
  • 03A messenger‑trust battery: testing who each segment already trusts to talk about a fast‑moving, difficult topic

Terms are defined in the glossary.

Published research

  • 01Bad, Bad, Not Good: How did the public respond to a caveman-speak explainer of AI risk? A coded analysis of 1,534 Instagram comments on a Nate Soares reel. Most commenters accepted the premise, agreement collapsed into resignation, and the one frame shared by believers and sceptics was blaming the companies.
  • 02OpenAI and Shut: how METR's investigation of the OpenAI and Hugging Face incident closed the marketing-stunt argument A coded analysis of 692 comments across the same Reddit ecosystem, before and after METR and Redwood Research's independent investigation. The marketing-stunt frame fell from a quarter of comments to one in twenty.
  • 03The clearest warning shot: how did the OpenAI agent-swarm story land with the audience most likely to believe it? A coded analysis of 1,245 YouTube comments on the Dwarkesh Podcast's interview with METR's Ajeya Cotra. The audience accepted the danger, joked about it at scale, praised the messenger, and still had nowhere to put its fear.
  • 04'A problem they created': how did the accountability version of the OpenAI / Hugging Face incident land with a news audience? A coded analysis of 283 YouTube comments on Amanpour and Company's interview with Heidy Khlaaf. A mainstream audience arrived already persuaded, angry rather than afraid, and asking for regulation without being prompted.
  • 05Findings: what four comment-thread studies say about explaining AI risk Nine patterns that hold across at least two of the four published studies, each stated as a hypothesis with the base it was measured on. Rewritten as studies are added.

Pilot study

We're deciding what counts as success before we start, not after. The pilot works if message performance clearly differs between value segments, if other people can repeat the method, and if at least five organisations agree to use the findings in their own communication. A null result counts too, since it would correct the field's assumptions and is still worth publishing.

GeographyUS, pilot phase
Sample~2,000 adults
Timeline~6 months from funding
  • 01A values‑segmented dataset on how audiences respond to claims about AI
  • 02A first map of which trusted messengers reach which segments
  • 03A replicable method, documented for others to run
  • 04An openly published findings report

Where the research programme goes next.

Once the pilot proves the method, the plan is to scale it: flagship segmentation studies that go deeper than the first pilot, and a live public‑attitudes tracker that keeps the evidence current as the technology and the public conversation move.
