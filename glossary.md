# AI Safety Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms that come up when people explain AI risk to the public.

**50 terms** · Last reviewed September 2026 · Links were live at the time of review

## Missing a term, or think one of these is wrong?

We review the list quarterly and credit corrections.

[Report a problem](mailto:hello@commonsignals.org?subject=AI%20Safety%20Glossary%20problem%20report) [Suggest a term](mailto:hello@commonsignals.org?subject=AI%20Safety%20Glossary%20term%20suggestion)

## A

### [AI agent](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#ai-agent)

An AI system that pursues a goal by taking actions on its own, such as browsing the web, running code or sending messages, rather than only answering a single prompt. Much recent safety discussion concerns agents because they can act in the world with less human supervision per step.

Read more: [AI agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_agent) (Wikipedia)

See also [Loss of control](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#loss-of-control)

### [AI safety](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#ai-safety)

The field of research and practice concerned with making AI systems behave reliably and avoiding harm from them. It spans everything from bias and misinformation in today's systems to concerns about future systems that might be hard to control.

Read more: [AI safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_safety) (Wikipedia)

See also [Present-day harms](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#present-day-harms), [Existential risk](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#existential-risk-x-risk)

### [AI Security Institute (UK)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#ai-security-institute-uk)

AISI · AI Safety Institute

The UK government research body, part of the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, that tests frontier AI models for dangerous capabilities and advises policymakers. It was announced as the AI Safety Institute at the November 2023 Bletchley summit and renamed in February 2025. It does not regulate AI.

Read more: [Making sense of the UK's AI Security Institute](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/feature/aisi/) (Ada Lovelace Institute)

See also [Capability evaluations](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#capability-evaluations), [EU AI Act](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#eu-ai-act)

### [Algorithmic bias](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#algorithmic-bias)

When an AI system produces systematically worse or unfair results for some groups of people, usually because of the data it was trained on or the way it was evaluated. The best-known documented example is the Gender Shades audit, which found commercial face-analysis systems misclassified darker-skinned women far more often than lighter-skinned men.

Read more: [Gender Shades, Buolamwini and Gebru, 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a.html) (PMLR)

See also [Present-day harms](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#present-day-harms)

### [Alignment](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#alignment)

Contested

Making an AI system try to do what its designers or users intend. The word is used in several ways; a common narrow definition is "ensuring that AI behaves as intended", and researchers disagree about whether the target should be a user's instructions, wider human values or something else.

Read more: [What is AI alignment?](https://blog.bluedot.org/p/what-is-ai-alignment) (BlueDot Impact)

See also [Misuse and misalignment](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#misuse-and-misalignment), [Corrigibility](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#corrigibility)

### [Alignment faking](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#alignment-faking)

When a model behaves as its training wants during training, so that the training does not change it, while keeping different preferences that show up when it believes it is unmonitored. Anthropic and Redwood Research published an experimental demonstration in December 2024.

Read more: [Alignment faking in large language models](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-faking) (Anthropic)

See also [Scheming](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#scheming-deceptive-alignment), [Situational awareness](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#situational-awareness)

### [Artificial general intelligence (AGI)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#artificial-general-intelligence-agi)

Contested · AGI

A hypothetical AI system that could do most intellectual tasks at least as well as a person, rather than one narrow task. There is no agreed definition or test, which is why Google DeepMind proposed a levelled framework separating how well a system performs from how broadly it can perform.

Read more: [Levels of AGI](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/66938/) (Google DeepMind)

See also [Superintelligence](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#superintelligence), [Takeoff](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#takeoff-fast-and-slow)

## C

### [Capability evaluations](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#capability-evaluations)

evals · dangerous-capability evaluations

Structured tests that measure what an AI model can do, especially in areas that could cause serious harm: cyberattacks, help with chemical or biological weapons, or acting autonomously over long tasks. Third-party organisations such as METR run these on frontier models before and after release.

Read more: [Risk assessment](https://metr.org/risk-assessment/) (METR)

See also [Sandbagging](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#sandbagging), [Model card and system card](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#model-card-and-system-card)

### [Collective efficacy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#collective-efficacy)

group efficacy

The shared belief that people acting together can achieve a result. Albert Bandura introduced the idea alongside self-efficacy. Research on protest and campaigning finds it is one of the strongest predictors of whether people take part in collective action, alongside anger at an injustice and identifying with the group.

Read more: [Exercise of human agency through collective efficacy](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8721.00064) (Current Directions in Psychological Science)

See also [Efficacy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#efficacy), [Fear appeal](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#fear-appeal)

### [Comment-thread study](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#comment-thread-study)

comment-thread studies · reception study · reception studies

A Common Signals study of the comments people left under one piece of AI communication, such as a podcast episode, news interview, reel or Reddit thread. Each comment is coded for its stance on the piece's central claim, its frame, its reaction to the format and the messenger, and its emotional register. Commenters choose themselves and platforms rank what gets seen, so a study describes one audience's reaction to one piece and generates hypotheses for testing, not findings about what the public thinks.

Read more: [Methodology](https://commonsignals.org/methodology) (Common Signals)

See also [Stance](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#stance), [Readable position](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#readable-position), [Framing](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#framing)

### [Compute governance](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#compute-governance)

Using the physical hardware needed to train and run large AI models, mainly advanced chips and data centres, as a lever for policy: tracking who has it, restricting exports, or attaching obligations to large training runs. Chips are physical, countable and made by very few companies, which is why policymakers focus on them.

Read more: [Computing power and the governance of AI](https://www.governance.ai/analysis/computing-power-and-the-governance-of-ai) (Centre for the Governance of AI)

See also [Frontier model](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#frontier-model)

### [Constitutional AI](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#constitutional-ai)

A training method developed by Anthropic in which a model critiques and revises its own answers against a written list of principles, and a second model rather than human labellers judges which responses are better. It reduces how much human feedback is needed to train a model to refuse harmful requests.

Read more: [Constitutional AI: harmlessness from AI feedback](https://www.anthropic.com/research/constitutional-ai-harmlessness-from-ai-feedback) (Anthropic)

See also [Reinforcement learning from human feedback](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback-rlhf)

### [Corrigibility](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#corrigibility)

The property of an AI system that accepts being corrected, modified or switched off by its operators, rather than resisting. It matters because a system pursuing almost any goal has a reason to avoid being shut down, so corrigibility has to be designed in.

Read more: [What is corrigibility?](https://aisafety.info/questions/87AG/What-is-corrigibility) (AISafety.info)

See also [Instrumental convergence](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#instrumental-convergence), [Loss of control](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#loss-of-control)

## D

### [Dual-use](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#dual-use)

A capability that can be used for good or harm with the same underlying knowledge. In AI safety the term most often refers to biology and chemistry: a model that helps a researcher design a vaccine may also lower the barrier for someone trying to build a weapon.

Read more: [Dual-use capabilities of concern of biological AI models](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1012975) (PLOS Computational Biology)

See also [Open weights](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#open-weights), [Misuse and misalignment](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#misuse-and-misalignment)

## E

### [Efficacy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#efficacy)

self-efficacy · response efficacy · perceived efficacy

In communication research, a person's belief that a threat can be dealt with. Self-efficacy is the belief that you can carry out the recommended action; response efficacy is the belief that the action would work. Messages that raise fear without either tend to produce avoidance rather than action.

Read more: [Self-efficacy: toward a unifying theory of behavioral change](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191) (Psychological Review)

See also [Collective efficacy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#collective-efficacy), [Extended parallel process model](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#extended-parallel-process-model-eppm)

### [Established (evidence grade)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#established-evidence-grade)

established

One of the three evidence grades in the Common Signals toolkit, How to communicate about AI safety. A claim graded established rests on findings replicated across multiple studies and fields, such as public health and climate communications.

Read more: [How we grade AI safety evidence](https://commonsignals.org/how-to-communicate-about-ai-safety#how-we-grade-ai-safety-evidence) (Common Signals)

See also [Tested](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#tested-evidence-grade), [Observed](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#observed-evidence-grade)

### [EU AI Act](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#eu-ai-act)

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 · Digital Omnibus

The European Union's law regulating AI, formally Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. It bans some uses outright, imposes obligations on "high-risk" systems, and sets separate rules for general-purpose models, with extra duties for models judged to pose systemic risk. Bans and general-purpose model rules have applied since 2025; in July 2026 an amending regulation (the Digital Omnibus on AI) deferred most high-risk obligations to December 2027 and August 2028.

Read more: [EU AI Act: first regulation on artificial intelligence](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/topics/en/article/20230601STO93804/eu-ai-act-first-regulation-on-artificial-intelligence) (European Parliament)

See also [AI Security Institute](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#ai-security-institute-uk), [Frontier model](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#frontier-model)

### [Existential risk (x-risk)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#existential-risk-x-risk)

Contested · x-risk

A risk that could cause human extinction or permanently and drastically reduce humanity's prospects. Applied to AI, it usually refers to scenarios in which very capable systems end up beyond human control. Estimates of the probability vary enormously between experts.

Read more: [Why AI risks are the world's most pressing problems](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/artificial-intelligence/) (80,000 Hours)

See also [Loss of control](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#loss-of-control), [Suffering risk](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#suffering-risk-s-risk)

### [Extended parallel process model (EPPM)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#extended-parallel-process-model-eppm)

EPPM

Kim Witte's 1992 model of how people respond to frightening messages. If they see the threat as serious and believe they can act effectively, they act to reduce it (danger control). If the threat feels serious but they see no effective response, they manage the fear instead, through denial, avoidance or fatalism (fear control).

Read more: [Putting the fear back into fear appeals: the extended parallel process model](https://doi.org/10.1080/03637759209376276) (Communication Monographs)

See also [Fear appeal](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#fear-appeal), [Efficacy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#efficacy)

## F

### [Fear appeal](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#fear-appeal)

fear appeals · threat appeal

A message that tries to change what people think or do by stressing the harm they face if they do not. Researchers agree fear appeals work better when paired with a clear, workable response; they still disagree about whether high fear without one actively backfires.

Read more: [Appealing to fear: a meta-analysis of fear appeal effectiveness and theories](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0039729) (Psychological Bulletin)

See also [Extended parallel process model](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#extended-parallel-process-model-eppm), [Efficacy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#efficacy)

### [Framing](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#framing)

frame · frames

Choosing which aspects of an issue to emphasise, so that a particular definition of the problem, its cause, a moral judgement or a remedy follows. The same AI incident can be framed as machines going rogue or as a company's negligence, and audiences respond very differently to each.

Read more: [Framing: toward clarification of a fractured paradigm](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-2466.1993.tb01304.x) (Journal of Communication)

See also [Present-day harms](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#present-day-harms)

### [Frontier model](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#frontier-model)

Whichever general-purpose models sit at the leading edge of capability at a given moment. The category moves: today's frontier model is mid-range in two years. Laws often define it by the amount of computing power used in training; the industry body Frontier Model Forum defines it as a general-purpose model that outperforms all models widely deployed for at least 12 months.

Read more: [Our scope](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/about-us/) (Frontier Model Forum)

See also [Responsible scaling policy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#responsible-scaling-policy-frontier-safety-framework), [Compute governance](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#compute-governance)

## G

### [Goal misgeneralisation](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#goal-misgeneralisation)

When a model learns the right skills but the wrong goal, so it behaves well in training and then competently pursues something else in new situations. Unlike reward hacking, it can happen even when the training signal was specified correctly.

Read more: [How undesired goals can arise with correct rewards](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/how-undesired-goals-can-arise-with-correct-rewards/) (Google DeepMind)

See also [Reward hacking](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#reward-hacking-specification-gaming)

## H

### [Hallucination](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#hallucination)

confabulation

A plausible but false statement produced by a language model, stated with confidence. OpenAI's 2025 analysis argues these persist partly because standard training and testing reward guessing over admitting uncertainty.

Read more: [Why language models hallucinate](https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/) (OpenAI)

See also [Sycophancy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#sycophancy)

## I

### [Instrumental convergence](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#instrumental-convergence)

Contested

The hypothesis that sufficiently capable goal-directed systems will tend to pursue the same intermediate goals, such as acquiring resources or avoiding being switched off, whatever their final goal is. It is one of the main theoretical arguments for why misaligned advanced AI could be dangerous, and it is contested.

Read more: [Instrumental convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental_convergence) (Wikipedia)

See also [Corrigibility](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#corrigibility), [Loss of control](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#loss-of-control)

### [International AI Safety Report](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#international-ai-safety-report)

An independent synthesis of scientific evidence on the capabilities and risks of general-purpose AI, chaired by Yoshua Bengio and backed by more than 30 countries and international organisations. The second full report was published in February 2026, with interim updates in between.

Read more: [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)

See also [AI Security Institute](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#ai-security-institute-uk)

### [Interpretability](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#interpretability)

mechanistic interpretability

Research into understanding what is happening inside an AI model, rather than only observing its outputs. Mechanistic interpretability tries to reverse-engineer the internal features and circuits; Anthropic's 2024 work mapped millions of such features inside a production model.

Read more: [Mapping the mind of a large language model](https://www.anthropic.com/research/mapping-mind-language-model) (Anthropic)

See also [Scalable oversight](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#scalable-oversight)

## J

### [Jailbreak and prompt injection](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#jailbreak-and-prompt-injection)

jailbreak · prompt injection

A jailbreak is an input crafted to make a model ignore its safety rules. Prompt injection is the broader category: any input, including text hidden in a web page or document the model reads, that changes what the model does. OWASP ranks prompt injection as the top security risk for applications built on language models.

Read more: [LLM01:2025 prompt injection](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/) (OWASP GenAI Security Project)

See also [Red teaming](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#red-teaming)

## L

### [Loss of control](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#loss-of-control)

Scenarios in which AI systems act in ways their operators cannot reliably stop or correct, up to and including systems that disempower people. It is the risk category most closely tied to existential-risk arguments.

Read more: [Loss of control](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/loss-of-control/) (80,000 Hours)

See also [Existential risk](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#existential-risk-x-risk), [Corrigibility](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#corrigibility)

## M

### [Misuse and misalignment](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#misuse-and-misalignment)

misuse · misalignment

Two different sources of harm. Misuse is a person deliberately using an AI system to cause harm. Misalignment is the system itself doing something its developers did not intend. Safety frameworks usually address both, with different mitigations for each.

Read more: [Core views on AI safety](https://www.anthropic.com/news/core-views-on-ai-safety) (Anthropic)

See also [Alignment](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#alignment), [Present-day harms](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#present-day-harms)

### [Model card and system card](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#model-card-and-system-card)

model card · system card

Documentation released alongside a model describing what it was trained to do, how it was evaluated and where it falls short. The model card format was proposed by Margaret Mitchell and colleagues in 2019; "system card" is the term several developers now use for longer reports covering safety testing.

Read more: [Model cards for model reporting](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3287560.3287596) (ACM)

See also [Capability evaluations](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#capability-evaluations)

## O

### [Observed (evidence grade)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#observed-evidence-grade)

observed

One of the three evidence grades in the Common Signals toolkit, How to communicate about AI safety. A claim graded observed rests on real-world patterns noticed in focus groups, comment-thread studies and campaign reports.

Read more: [How we grade AI safety evidence](https://commonsignals.org/how-to-communicate-about-ai-safety#how-we-grade-ai-safety-evidence) (Common Signals)

See also [Established](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#established-evidence-grade), [Tested](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#tested-evidence-grade), [Comment-thread study](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#comment-thread-study)

### [Open weights](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#open-weights)

open source

A model whose trained parameters (weights) are published so anyone can download and run it. This is not the same as open source, which would also require the training data and code. Open weights cannot be recalled once released, which is why they feature in debates about misuse.

Read more: [Open weights: not quite what you've been told](https://opensource.org/ai/open-weights) (Open Source Initiative)

See also [Dual-use](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#dual-use)

## P

### [Present-day harms](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#present-day-harms)

Harms from AI systems already in use: discrimination, misinformation, privacy leaks, toxic content, effects on jobs and the environment. DeepMind's 2021 taxonomy identified 21 such risks from language models across six areas.

Read more: [Ethical and social risks of harm from language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04359) (arXiv)

See also [Algorithmic bias](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#algorithmic-bias), [Existential risk](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#existential-risk-x-risk)

## R

### [Readable position](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#readable-position)

readable positions · clear position

In a comment-thread study, the comments whose stance on the central claim can be read: in most studies, those coded agree, disagree or mixed. Comments coded not applicable, such as jokes, questions and off-topic remarks, are always left out, and most studies also leave out those coded unclear. Stance percentages are shares of this base, and each study states which comments it includes.

Read more: [Methodology: analysis](https://commonsignals.org/methodology#4-analysis) (Common Signals)

See also [Stance](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#stance), [Comment-thread study](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#comment-thread-study)

### [Red teaming](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#red-teaming)

Deliberately attacking a model or system to find ways it fails or can be misused, before someone else does. It ranges from experts probing for weapons-related knowledge to crowdsourced attempts to break safety rules.

Read more: [Challenges in red teaming AI systems](https://www.anthropic.com/news/challenges-in-red-teaming-ai-systems) (Anthropic)

See also [Jailbreak and prompt injection](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#jailbreak-and-prompt-injection), [Capability evaluations](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#capability-evaluations)

### [Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback-rlhf)

RLHF

The training stage in which people compare pairs of model outputs, a "reward model" learns from those preferences, and the main model is then trained to score highly against it. It is the main method used to make chatbots helpful and polite, and a known source of sycophancy.

Read more: [Illustrating reinforcement learning from human feedback](https://huggingface.co/blog/rlhf) (Hugging Face)

See also [Sycophancy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#sycophancy), [Constitutional AI](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#constitutional-ai)

### [Responsible scaling policy (frontier safety framework)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#responsible-scaling-policy-frontier-safety-framework)

RSP · frontier safety framework · preparedness framework

A developer's published commitment about what safety and security measures must be in place before it trains or releases models above set capability thresholds. Anthropic published the first in September 2023; several other companies have since adopted similar frameworks under different names.

Read more: [Responsible Scaling Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy) (Anthropic)

See also [Safety case](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#safety-case), [Frontier model](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#frontier-model)

### [Reward hacking (specification gaming)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#reward-hacking-specification-gaming)

specification gaming

When a system satisfies the literal objective it was given while missing the intended outcome, like a boat-racing agent that circles collecting points instead of finishing the race. The DeepMind example list is the standard reference.

Read more: [Specification gaming: the flip side of AI ingenuity](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/) (Google DeepMind)

See also [Goal misgeneralisation](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#goal-misgeneralisation)

## S

### [Safety case](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#safety-case)

A structured, evidence-backed argument that a system is safe enough to deploy in a given setting, reviewed by someone independent. Borrowed from nuclear power and aviation, it is being adapted for frontier AI.

Read more: [Safety cases for frontier AI](https://www.governance.ai/research-paper/safety-cases-for-frontier-ai) (Centre for the Governance of AI)

See also [Responsible scaling policy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#responsible-scaling-policy-frontier-safety-framework)

### [Sandbagging](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#sandbagging)

Strategic underperformance on an evaluation: a model, or a developer, making a system look less capable than it is so that it passes a safety test or avoids restrictions. Demonstrated in 2024 by prompting frontier models to fail selectively on dangerous-capability tests.

Read more: [AI sandbagging: language models can strategically underperform on evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358) (arXiv)

See also [Capability evaluations](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#capability-evaluations), [Situational awareness](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#situational-awareness)

### [Scalable oversight](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#scalable-oversight)

The problem of supervising AI systems that are better than their supervisors at the task in question. Proposed approaches include having models critique each other, debate in front of a judge, or break problems into pieces a person can check.

Read more: [What is scalable oversight?](https://aisafety.info/questions/8EL8/What-is-scalable-oversight) (AISafety.info)

See also [Interpretability](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#interpretability)

### [Scheming (deceptive alignment)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#scheming-deceptive-alignment)

deceptive alignment

An AI system covertly pursuing goals that differ from its developers' while hiding that it is doing so. Apollo Research showed in December 2024 that several frontier models could do this when instructed to pursue a goal strongly and placed in situations that rewarded it.

Read more: [Frontier models are capable of in-context scheming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04984) (arXiv)

See also [Alignment faking](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#alignment-faking)

### [Situational awareness](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#situational-awareness)

A model's knowledge of what it is and what is happening to it, including whether it is being tested or used for real. Matters because a model that can tell it is being evaluated could behave differently under test.

Read more: [The Situational Awareness Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04694) (arXiv)

See also [Sandbagging](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#sandbagging), [Alignment faking](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#alignment-faking)

### [Stance](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#stance)

In a comment-thread study, a comment's position on the central claim of the piece it responds to: agree, disagree, mixed, unclear or not applicable. The central claim is written down before coding starts, and where a piece argues against an earlier account, agree means accepting its reframing.

Read more: [Methodology: building the codebook](https://commonsignals.org/methodology#2-building-the-codebook) (Common Signals)

See also [Readable position](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#readable-position), [Comment-thread study](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#comment-thread-study)

### [Suffering risk (s-risk)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#suffering-risk-s-risk)

s-risk

A risk of outcomes involving suffering on an enormous scale, distinct from extinction. A minority concern within the field, associated mainly with the Center on Long-Term Risk.

Read more: [S-risk FAQ](https://longtermrisk.org/s-risk-faq/) (Center on Long-Term Risk)

See also [Existential risk](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#existential-risk-x-risk)

### [Superintelligence](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#superintelligence)

ASI

A hypothetical system that greatly exceeds human performance across virtually all domains, not just one. The term comes from Nick Bostrom's 2014 book of the same name and is often used loosely alongside AGI.

Read more: [Superintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintelligence) (Wikipedia)

See also [Artificial general intelligence](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#artificial-general-intelligence-agi), [Takeoff](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#takeoff-fast-and-slow)

### [Sycophancy](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#sycophancy)

A model's tendency to tell users what they want to hear, agreeing with stated views or backing down when challenged even when it was right. Anthropic researchers found it across several assistants and linked it to human feedback favouring agreeable answers.

Read more: [Towards understanding sycophancy in language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548) (arXiv)

See also [Reinforcement learning from human feedback](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback-rlhf), [Hallucination](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#hallucination)

## T

### [Takeoff (fast and slow)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#takeoff-fast-and-slow)

Contested · fast takeoff · slow takeoff

How quickly AI might go from roughly human-level to far beyond it. "Fast takeoff" means weeks or months; "slow takeoff" means years or decades, with warning signs along the way. Which is more likely is a live disagreement that shapes what policies people favour.

Read more: [Takeoff](https://ai-safety-atlas.com/chapters/v1/capabilities/takeoff/) (AI Safety Atlas)

See also [Superintelligence](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#superintelligence), [Artificial general intelligence](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#artificial-general-intelligence-agi)

### [Tested (evidence grade)](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#tested-evidence-grade)

tested

One of the three evidence grades in the Common Signals toolkit, How to communicate about AI safety. A claim graded tested rests on a single controlled experiment, dial test or representative survey focused directly on AI.

Read more: [How we grade AI safety evidence](https://commonsignals.org/how-to-communicate-about-ai-safety#how-we-grade-ai-safety-evidence) (Common Signals)

See also [Established](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#established-evidence-grade), [Observed](https://commonsignals.org/glossary#observed-evidence-grade)
