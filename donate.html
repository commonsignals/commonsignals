    Support our work

    Common Signals is a neutral research body. We study how the public receives real AI moments, package the evidence in a graded toolkit for the people who explain AI, and publish the hypotheses it raises for others to test. Everything we produce is published free: the findings, the coded datasets, the codebooks and the method.

    Common Signals is not yet funded. A donation would pay for research that would not otherwise exist, and keep it open for anyone to use.

    What your donation funds

    A donation of any size goes directly into research and its publication. Here is what one study costs.

    $500

    Funds a comment-reception study: several hundred to a few thousand public comments on a high-profile AI moment, coded against a published codebook, human spot-checked, and released with the full dataset in our data explorer. Our studies of the Nate Soares reel, the METR report and the Ajeya Cotra episode were produced this way.

    Why fund research on AI communication

    AI safety has technical research, governance work, funders and training programmes. It has no shared evidence base on how to talk to the public. The people explaining AI, whether their concern is present-day harms or existential risk, mostly work from instinct. The little evidence that exists, including a 2025 message-testing paper by Philip Trippenbach and colleagues at the Seismic Foundation, Blue State and the Centre for Future Generations, suggests the field's dominant framings often underperform.

    The climate movement had the same problem and built Climate Outreach to solve it. Britain Talks Climate, its values-based segmentation with More in Common and YouGov, produced free toolkits and training that campaigns across the movement rely on. The IPCC commissioned Climate Outreach to write the communications handbook for its own authors. Nobody has built the equivalent for AI.

    Common Signals is building the equivalent for AI, started early in the curve while the gap is widest. We study how the public receives real AI moments, package the evidence in a graded toolkit, and publish an open agenda of hypotheses for others to test. Our theory of change, Creating a social mandate for AI governance, sets out the thinking behind it.

    How we handle your money

    Neutrality is the point of Common Signals, so we are careful about where funding comes from and how it is used.

    Donations are ring-fenced as untied research funding. Donors do not choose research questions or see findings before publication. We will publish our funder list once we have funders, and our long-term plan caps any single funder at 25% of income. A clean null result is published exactly like a positive one.

    How to give

    To give, email hello@commonsignals.org and we will reply with the ways to pay.

    Common Signals is not yet a registered charity, so donations are not tax-deductible and are not eligible for Gift Aid. We will update this page when that changes.

    If you are considering a gift of $5,000 or more, or want to fund a specific study, say so in your email and we will send the current research plan and budget.

    Other ways to help

    Money is one of three things we ask for. The others are introductions to foundations that seed independent research institutes, and criticism from people who have run something adjacent and watched it work or fail. Both can be sent to hello@commonsignals.org.

    If you communicate about AI, the most useful thing you can do is use the findings. Everything on the research page is free to apply, adapt and cite.
