    Findings

    Our findings are now kept as the eleven hypotheses in the Common Signals research agenda. This page lists the six studies behind them and points each earlier finding to its hypothesis.

    View as markdown

    From 11 November 2026, the patterns this page used to list are maintained as the eleven hypotheses in the Common Signals research agenda, which says how often each has been seen, which four to settle first and how each could be tested. H1 to H9 keep this page's numbering. H10 and H11 come from the Diary of a CEO study.

    Each study codes the full comment thread under one piece of AI communication. None is a claim about what the public thinks: the studies generate hypotheses, and the agenda is where those hypotheses are kept.

    Where each finding went

    Links to the sections this page used to have still land here, and each points on to its place in the agenda.

    • 1. Acceptance is high everywhere. Now H1. Audiences that engage with AI risk content mostly accept the risk already.
    • 2. Persuasion does not produce action. Now H2. Persuaded audiences do not ask what to do.
    • 3. Threat without efficacy produced resignation. Now H3. The emotion a telling produces depends on what it supplies.
    • 4. A concrete incident removes the capability objection. Now H4. A documented incident removes the capability objection, and forecasts bring it back.
    • 5. Once capability is conceded, disagreement is about motive and messenger. Now H5. Once capability is conceded, disagreement is about motive and messenger.
    • 6. Independent verification moves sceptics. Now H6. Independent verification moves sceptics when the facts stay the same.
    • 7. A named, calm, trusted messenger is rewarded. Now H7. A named, calm, credentialed messenger is rewarded, and the credential is attacked first.
    • 8. Accountability is the strongest bridging-frame hypothesis. Now H8. Blaming the people and companies in charge is a frame believers and sceptics share.
    • 9. Humour aids distribution. Now H9. Humour spreads a story without lowering agreement, and does not carry its conclusion.
    • What is new. The measurements it listed are now the evidence under each hypothesis, H1 to H11.
    • What we would test next. Now Priorities for 2027 and How to test each hypothesis.
    • Method and limits. Now the methodology page and the agenda's standard a study must meet.

    Data and citation

    Codebooks and anonymised coded datasets for each study are published in our data explorer, browsable comment by comment: the Soares reel, Reddit before and after the METR report, the Cotra episode, the Amanpour and Company segment, the Coxon resignation thread, and the Diary of a CEO debate on YouTube, YouTube Shorts, Facebook, TikTok and Instagram. Re-codings and criticism are wanted.
