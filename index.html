    Common Signals helps people explaining AI see how their messages
    actually land.

    We run reception studies of real AI moments, package the evidence in a graded toolkit, and publish the hypotheses they raise for others to test.

    We focus on these questions

    How do real audiences receive the big moments in the AI story? What do they take from them?

    What makes AI communication backfire or fail to land? What can communicators learn from it?

    How strong is the evidence behind each piece of advice? Is it established, tested or only observed?

    Which open questions should be tested next, and who is best placed to test them?

    "Can't we just unplug it?": The Diary of a CEO AI debate analysed through 26,000 comments

    We coded over 26,000 comments on the Diary of a CEO's AI risk debate: the full episode and three shorter cuts of it, posted across five platforms. Agreement with the risk case ran from 88% under a 21-minute cut on Facebook to 49% under a 104-second trailer on YouTube Shorts.

    Why Common Signals

    Global AI investment reached around $581.7bn in 2025 [1]. A sliver of that goes to safety. A sliver of the sliver goes to helping the public understand any of it.

    $581.7bnglobal AI investment, 2025

    Grounded in evidence

    The people trying to explain AI mostly work from instinct. Early evidence, including a 2025 message-testing paper by Philip Trippenbach and colleagues at the Seismic Foundation, Blue State and the Centre for Future Generations [2], suggests the field's dominant framings underperform.

    Neutral by design

    No policy position, no campaigns, no proprietary advice. Findings go to every side of the AI conversation, which is what lets one body credibly serve both.

    A lesson from climate, applied early

    Climate Outreach showed that shared, neutral evidence on how the public hears an issue can serve a whole movement. We are applying that lesson to AI while the gap is still wide open.
