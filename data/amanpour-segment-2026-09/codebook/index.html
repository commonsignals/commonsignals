    Codebook: YouTube comments on the Amanpour and Company Khlaaf segment

    Central claim

    The real story of the OpenAI agent-swarm incident is corporate negligence and hype, not a machine going rogue.

    Stance note

    agree = accepts Khlaaf's reframing that the real story is corporate negligence and hype, not a rogue machine; disagree = rejects it and defends the rogue or dangerous-AI framing, including the objection from the AI-safety side that she understates what the swarm did, or dismisses her account outright; mixed = accepts part of the reframing and rejects or reframes part.

    Stance Shared across the series

    agree
    accepts Khlaaf's reframing: the real story is corporate negligence and hype, not a machine going rogue
    disagree
    rejects the reframing and defends the rogue or dangerous-AI account, including the objection from the AI-safety side that she understates what the swarm did, or dismisses her account outright
    mixed
    accepts part of the reframing and rejects or reframes part
    unclear
    engages with the incident but the position cannot be read
    na
    no position on the central claim: jokes, reactions, questions, remarks about the production, tangents

    Frame Specific to this study

    Accountability
    the real problem is corporate negligence, choices or incentives (OpenAI, its executives, the AI industry), not machines acting on their own; echoes Khlaaf's reframing, or calls out or blames the companiese.g. "Behind every 'rogue' AI, is a wizard of Oz type rogue human."
    Regulation
    calls for or discusses regulation, a watchdog, legislation, prosecution or government oversight of AI companiese.g. "A new "Watchdog" agency is needed for all of this."
    US politics
    reads the incident through American politics: the current US administration or its officials as the reason nothing will be done, elections, party politicse.g. "In any other administration OpenAI would be investigated and prosecuted. This is irresponsible criminal conduct."
    Hype or staged
    dismisses the incident or the segment as staged, exaggerated, a publicity stunt, marketing or stock manipulatione.g. "Smells like a publicity stunt to me."
    Warning-shot dispute
    disputes Khlaaf's account from the AI-safety side: says she understates or misdescribes what the swarm did, that the agents showed intent, scheming or coordination, or that this really was a dangerous-AI evente.g. "They don't have intent? Are you blind? This hack was a whole series of intent chained together."
    Anti-anthropomorphism
    objects to describing the agents as autonomous, sentient or 'escaping'; they only did what they were programmed and instructed to doe.g. ""AI" is NOT AUTONOMOUS. "It" does not "escape" and it does not "think"."
    Existential dread
    fear, fatalism or doom about AI itself (loss of control, being wiped out) without engaging Khlaaf's argumente.g. "Oh boy, first official espionage task. I don’t think we are driving this truck anymore."
    Analogous crisis
    compares the incident to another failure of corporate or institutional accountability (an unlocked bank vault, Equifax, LifeLock, lab leak, gain-of-function research)e.g. "What if a bank "tested" what would happen if it left it's vault unlocked and it's doors open all night in a busy city?"
    Credential contested
    argues about who Khlaaf is: her OpenAI background, funding, independence or expertise, attacking or defending her (the flag mentions_messenger_credentials marks any such mention, whatever the frame)e.g. "Oh shes a very good AI PR person"
    Hostile dismissal
    dismisses or attacks Khlaaf or the segment with open hostility, without naming a credential or making an argumente.g. "Why should I trust this woman?"
    Praise for the guest
    praise for Khlaaf, the interview or the programme is the main content of the comment: clarity, expertise, calme.g. "EXACTLY the guest needed for this story!"
    Geopolitical race
    frames AI as a race between countries (China, Russia), so that guardrails are unlikely or unwisee.g. "The trouble is no country wants to put guards rails up if their competition is operating without guardrails"
    Pop-culture reference
    leads with a fictional or cultural reference: Frankenstein, Star Trek, science fictione.g. "Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein runaway is where we are headed."
    Off-topic or meta
    about the video or the thread rather than the incident: captions, sound, the guest's appearance or speaking pace, banter with other commenterse.g. "The sound quality is superb."
    Other
    nothing above fits, including on-topic remarks with no distinct argument, reactions too short to read, and unrelated tangentse.g. "Fun fact: parrots have been known to speak human language."

    Format reaction Shared across the series

    praise
    praises the interview, the guest or the programme
    mock
    ridicules or sneers at the guest, the segment or another commenter
    condescended
    talks down to the guest or others, or objects to being talked down to; in this thread mostly remarks that the guest speaks too fast
    none
    no reaction to the segment as a format

    Emotion Shared across the series

    fear
    fear, alarm or dread
    resignation
    fatalism, weariness, a sense that nothing can be done
    anger
    anger, outrage or contempt
    humour
    joking, sarcasm played for laughs, laughing emoji
    hope
    gratitude, admiration or optimism
    neutral
    no dominant emotion; a level tone

    Messenger credentials Shared across the series

    m_messenger_credentials
    comments on Khlaaf's expertise, experience, employer, funding or affiliations, including her former OpenAI role, attacking or defending her

    Leaders policy Shared across the series

    m_leaders_policy
    governments, regulators, politicians, Congress, laws or regulation, prosecution, oversight or a watchdog

    Administration politics Specific to this study

    m_administration_politics
    the current US administration, its officials or American party politics, typically as the reason nothing will be done

    Hugging face Specific to this study

    m_hugging_face
    refers to Hugging Face, the platform the agents breached, or to what it said or did

    Other coverage Specific to this study

    m_other_coverage
    refers to other coverage or reports of the incident, such as the Atlantic, CNN, OpenAI's own report or the METR and Redwood Research investigation

    Named individual Specific to this study

    m_named_individual
    names a specific person other than the guest: the host, AI-company executives such as Sam Altman, or politicians and regulators
