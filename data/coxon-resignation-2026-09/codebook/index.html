    Codebook: X replies to Jacob Coxon's Anthropic resignation thread

    Central claim

    Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic is acting responsibly with frontier AI, and both are racing towards dangerous self-improving superintelligence.

    Stance note

    agree = accepts that neither OpenAI nor Anthropic is acting responsibly and that racing towards self-improving superintelligence is a danger worth resigning over; disagree = rejects this, on any grounds (capability scepticism, geopolitics, IPO cynicism, or defending the labs); mixed = accepts part of the danger claim while rejecting or reframing another part, for example disputing that resigning was the right response.

    Stance Shared across the series

    Agree
    accepts that neither company is acting responsibly and the danger described is real
    Disagree
    rejects this, on any grounds
    Mixed
    accepts part, rejects or reframes part
    Na
    on topic but no readable position
    Unclear
    genuinely cannot be told either way; used sparingly

    Frame Specific to this study

    Capability scepticism
    doubts current or near-term AI can do what Coxon describese.g. "We aren't going to die out because a token prediction model gained sentience. Get a grip."
    China race
    argues unilateral restraint just hands the advantage to China or another geopolitical rival
    Format only
    reacts only to the thread's tone, length or format rather than its contente.g. "Well this was a pleasant read right before bed."
    General agreement
    endorsement without a distinct argument
    Technical argument
    makes a substantive technical claim about how the danger would or wouldn't unfolde.g. "Imagine the next big war isn't kinetic, it doesn't involve bombs or guns. It doesn't even involve people - it's between AI models."
    Blame people
    accepts the danger is real but locates the fault in human greed, elitism or the desire for control rather than the technology itselfe.g. "This is exactly why AI needs to be people-owned, not controlled by a handful of billionaires."
    How to act
    asks or proposes what an ordinary person, or anyone, should actually do in responsee.g. "Can someone explain what we non-tech non-billionaire non-bunker owning people are supposed to do with this information?"
    Scifi
    frames the claim through film, book, game or other fiction
    Hypocrisy critique
    argues that quitting is the wrong response if Coxon genuinely believes what he sayse.g. "if you really believe this you stay on the job and sabotage the progress, what does resigning do?"
    Messenger authenticity
    questions or defends whether Coxon's resignation and stated motives are genuine, as opposed to arguing about the danger claim itselfe.g. "Be real bro you did it for the Aella incentive and aura farming points."
    Ipo marketing cynicism
    reads the whole thread as promotional and timed to Anthropic's pending IPOe.g. "okay so buy the IPO?"
    Fatalism
    accepts the danger but responds with resignation or nihilism rather than a call to acte.g. "oh, so we're fucking fucked"
    Conspiracy or other target
    reframes the thread as a paid, staged or PR operation for Anthropic, OpenAI or another party, rather than a sincere resignatione.g. "wait bro this is the latest round of Anthropic PR lol can't believe I didn't see it at first."
    Religion or spiritual
    frames the danger, or Coxon's role, in religious or spiritual termse.g. "If AI apocalypse is our downfall then that is gods will."
    Present harms
    argues the more pressing danger is nearer-term harm, such as jobs or economic disruption, rather than existential riske.g. "either we think this technology kills us all, or we think it takes all of our jobs"
    Benefits
    argues AI's benefits outweigh the risk described, or that stopping or slowing development is the real dangere.g. "We will not throw away a utopian paradise for a small chance of it not working."
    Other
    doesn't fit any of the above, including off-topic tangents and undifferentiated hostility

    Format reaction Shared across the series

    None
    no distinct reaction to the thread as a piece of writing, beyond the content argument itself
    Mock
    mocks or ridicules the thread, Coxon, or the situation, rather than engaging its content
    Condescended
    responds with a condescending or patronising tone towards Coxon
    Imitates
    imitates or parodies Coxon's writing style or voice
    Praise
    praises Coxon, the thread, or the decision to write and post it, as a piece of communication

    Emotion Shared across the series

    Neutral
    dominant emotional register: neutral
    Fear
    dominant emotional register: fear
    Anger
    dominant emotional register: anger
    Resignation
    dominant emotional register: resignation
    Humour
    dominant emotional register: humour
    Hope
    dominant emotional register: hope

    Messenger credentials Shared across the series

    mentions_messenger_credentials
    names Coxon's own career history, credentials, or trustworthiness as the messenger, attacking or defending them

    Leaders policy Shared across the series

    mentions_leaders_policy
    governments, regulators, politicians, treaties, courts, law

    Companies billionaires Shared across the series

    mentions_companies_billionaires
    names companies, investors, billionaires, bubble, IPO

    Cultural reference Shared across the series

    mentions_cultural_reference
    references any film, book, game or fictional AI/robot

    Asks what to do Shared across the series

    mentions_asks_what_to_do
    asks or proposes what should be done

    China Specific to this study

    mentions_china
    mentions China, or geopolitical AI-race dynamics, specifically

    Ipo Specific to this study

    mentions_ipo
    specifically invokes Anthropic's pending IPO, or reads the thread as promotional or timed to it (narrower than mentions_companies_billionaires, which covers company and investor mentions generally)

    Non english Shared across the series

    non_english
    mostly not in English
