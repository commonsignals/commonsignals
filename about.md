# About

An independent, neutral evidence base for public understanding of AI.

## A public that understands AI well enough to shape it.

Vision

A world where the public understands AI well enough to shape it, not just live with what it becomes.

Mission

We build the shared, neutral evidence base for public understanding of AI: tested messages, mapped messengers and trained communicators, published openly for everyone working on this.

## Theory of change

Our full theory of change sets out why public engagement is critical to durable AI governance, and how we plan to help build the social mandate for it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does Common Signals actually do?**

We find out which messages about AI get through to which audiences, and which messengers those audiences trust, then publish what we find for anyone to use. The work has three parts: research (values-based audience segmentation, message testing and messenger mapping), a free toolkit built from the findings, and training and workshops that help communicators apply them.

**Who is this for?**

People whose job involves explaining AI to the public: campaigners, communications leads, spokespeople, policy teams, journalists and researchers. That includes organisations focused on present-day harms and organisations focused on existential risk. Both work from the same evidence base.

**Is Common Signals campaigning for a particular view of AI?**

No. We take no position on which rules are right, which risks matter most, or which organisations have it right. We do not run campaigns, lobby, or sell closed advice. Findings are released to everyone at the same time. That neutrality is what makes the research usable across a divided field, so we treat it as a design constraint rather than a preference.

**Why does this need to exist?**

Global AI investment reached around $581.7bn in 2025 (Stanford HAI, 2026 AI Index), and a very small share of that goes to safety, let alone to public understanding. The people explaining AI to the public mostly work from instinct, and the early evidence, including a 2025 message-testing paper by Philip Trippenbach and colleagues at the Seismic Foundation, Blue State and the Centre for Future Generations, suggests the field's dominant framings underperform. Climate communication went through the same problem twenty years ago and solved part of it with shared, neutral audience research. We are applying that model to AI while the gap is still open.

**What has been published so far?**

Six comment-thread studies, each coding the public response to one piece of AI communication: an Instagram reel explaining AI risk (1,534 comments), the Dwarkesh Podcast interview with METR's Ajeya Cotra (1,245), Amanpour and Company's interview with Heidy Khlaaf (322), a before-and-after study of Reddit's reaction to the OpenAI and Hugging Face incident (516), X replies to Jacob Coxon's resignation from Anthropic (2,480) and the Diary of a CEO debate on AI extinction risk across five platforms (26,019). We have also published a toolkit for communicators, How to communicate about AI safety, and a reading list of existing research, as well as the theory of change and the methodology behind the studies. All of it is on the research page.

**How do I get involved?**

Email [hello@commonsignals.org](mailto:hello@commonsignals.org). We are looking for organisations that will use the findings, people with relevant data or research partnerships to offer, funders, and critics. The newsletter goes out roughly monthly.

**What does "neutral" mean in practice?**

Four things. We publish everything, including null results and findings that contradict what the field assumes. Our advisory group and, once incorporated, our board are balanced across the field's main perspectives. We publish our funder list and cap any single funder's share of income. And findings go to every side at once, never to one organisation first.

**Where does the model come from?**

Climate Outreach, and specifically its Britain Talks Climate programme, which segmented the British public by values rather than demographics and tested which climate messages and messengers worked with each segment. That research became shared infrastructure for the whole climate movement. We are doing the same for AI, and building on existing work rather than duplicating it: Public Voices in AI (Ada Lovelace Institute and Alan Turing Institute), More in Common's segmentation craft, and the Seismic Foundation's message testing.

**Why segment by values rather than demographics?**

Because values predict how people respond to a message better than age, income or location do. Two people in the same postcode can hear the same claim about AI in opposite ways depending on what they already believe about institutions, fairness and change. Segmenting by values lets a communicator pick the frame and the messenger that fit the audience they are trying to reach.

**How is Common Signals funded?**

It is pre-funding. The pilot is being pitched to seed funders now, with early grants ring-fenced as untied research funding. The longer plan is a mix of grants from neutral trusts, a diversified funder base with no single funder above 25% of income, and a small line of earned income from workshops and message reviews, always under an open-methodology rule.

**Can I criticise the work?**

Please do. Errors, re-codings and methodological criticism are wanted. The codebooks and datasets are published so that anyone can check the coding and tell us where it is wrong. Email [hello@commonsignals.org](mailto:hello@commonsignals.org).
