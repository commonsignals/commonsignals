    How to communicate about AI safety.

    Our toolkit for anyone explaining AI risks to the public: ten principles, four ground rules and seven issue guides, with every claim graded by the strength of its evidence as established, tested or observed. Free and open.

    Read the toolkit →

    Built from the research.

    The toolkit is where the research goes. It brings together our reception studies and other people's published research, and grades every claim so you can see how far to trust it.

    • 01Advice graded by its evidence: established when it has been replicated across studies and fields, tested when it comes from a single controlled experiment or representative survey on AI, observed when it comes from comment threads, focus groups or campaign reports
    • 02Do's and don'ts for common claims about AI, each with the evidence behind it
    • 03Issue guides with example messages, starting with how to talk about loss of control

    Anyone talking about AI who wants to stop guessing.

    Campaigners and communicators across both the present‑day‑harms and existential‑risk conversations, policy teams drafting public‑facing material, journalists, and researchers who need their findings translated into something usable.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is in the toolkit?

    Ten principles, four ground rules and seven issue guides for explaining AI risks to the public, with every claim graded by the strength of its evidence: established, tested or observed. It draws on our reception studies and on research others have published, including Diffusion's six UK mindsets. Alongside it are the messenger map, the comment analyser and the data explorer. All of it is free and open.

    Is it available now?

    Yes. How to communicate about AI safety was published in November 2026, followed by its first stand-alone issue guide, on loss of control. Each grade shows how far the evidence goes, so you can see which advice is established and which has only been observed.

    Will it cost anything?

    No. The toolkit is free and stays free. That rule does not change as Common Signals grows.

    Who is it for?

    Anyone talking about AI who wants to stop guessing: campaigners and communicators on both the present-day-harms and existential-risk sides, policy teams, journalists and researchers who need findings translated into something a comms team can pick up and use.

    How is this different from advice I could get from a comms agency?

    It is built on published evidence rather than instinct, it is open rather than proprietary, and it is neutral: the guides tell you what worked with which audience, not what position to take. An agency can and should use it too.

    Will the toolkit be updated?

    Yes. Each new study adds to it, and the plan includes a live public-attitudes tracker so the guidance keeps pace as the technology and the public conversation change.
